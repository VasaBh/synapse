1. CORE ARCHITECTURE FUNDAMENTALS

A Senior Architect must demonstrate mastery of foundational principles and know when and why to apply each pattern.

1.1 Key Principles

  • CAP Theorem – Consistency, Availability, Partition Tolerance (pick any 2 in a distributed system)
  • SOLID – Single Responsibility, Open/Closed, Liskov, Interface Segregation, Dependency Inversion
  • 12-Factor App – methodology for building scalable, maintainable SaaS applications
  • DRY / KISS / YAGNI – avoid repetition, keep it simple, don't over-engineer
  • Conway's Law – system architecture mirrors communication structure of the organisation

1.2 Architecture Styles

StyleBest ForTrade-offs
MonolithSmall teams, rapid MVPHard to scale independently; tight coupling
MicroservicesLarge teams, independent deployabilityDistributed complexity; network overhead
Event-DrivenAsync workflows, high throughputEventual consistency; harder to debug
ServerlessBursty / unpredictable workloadsCold starts; vendor lock-in
CQRS+Event SrcAudit trails, complex domainsComplexity; read/write model sync

1.3 Design Patterns to Know

Creational / Structural

  • Factory, Abstract Factory, Builder, Singleton (use sparingly)
  • Adapter, Facade, Decorator, Proxy

Distributed Systems

  • Saga Pattern – manage distributed transactions without 2PC
  • Strangler Fig – incremental migration from monolith to microservices
  • Sidecar – offload cross-cutting concerns (logging, tracing) to companion process
  • Anti-Corruption Layer – protect domain model when integrating legacy systems
  • Bulkhead – isolate failures so one service doesn't sink the fleet

1.4 High Availability & Fault Tolerance

  • Active-Active vs Active-Passive failover
  • RPO (Recovery Point Objective) vs RTO (Recovery Time Objective)
  • Chaos Engineering – deliberately inject failures (Netflix Simian Army, LitmusChaos)
  • Graceful degradation – serve reduced functionality rather than hard failure
💡 Interview Tip: Always frame your answers around trade-offs. Never say 'X is always better than Y' — say 'X is preferable when… because…'
